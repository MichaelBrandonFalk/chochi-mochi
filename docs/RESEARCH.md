# Research and curriculum notes

Researched November 2, 2026. This is an original educational adaptation, not a reproduction of a SPACE manual, a treatment protocol, or an endorsed SPACE product. No clinical review or clinical efficacy testing of the game has occurred.

## Primary and authoritative sources

1. [Official SPACE program](https://www.spacetreatment.net/), developed by Eli Lebowitz. Establishes the parent-based approach and the dual focus on supportive responses and reducing accommodation.
2. [SPACE overview by Eli Lebowitz, International OCD Foundation](https://iocdf.org/about-ocd/ocd-treatment-guide/space/). Describes validation plus confidence, mapping accommodation, selecting and announcing adult changes, and trained professional delivery.
3. [Lebowitz et al., 2019 randomized noninferiority study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6732048/). Randomized 124 children ages 7–14 to parent-based SPACE or child CBT; 97 completed all sessions and assessments. SPACE was noninferior on the studied anxiety outcomes; accommodation decreased more in SPACE. This trial does not establish efficacy of this game, universal suitability, or equivalence for every family.

## Adaptation boundaries

- Children can learn kindness but are not responsible for treating a peer or parent. Glaloula need not become anxiety-free to finish the story.
- The game is not exposure therapy and assigns no real-world accommodation-reduction tasks to children. Caregivers lead real treatment plans.
- Keep appropriate medical, disability, sensory, developmental, and safety supports. Not all assistance is anxiety accommodation.
- Individual plans, pace, and difficult reactions need clinical judgment. A caregiver’s warm consistency is not permission for coercion or ignoring needs.
- Simple prose, short paragraphs, and concrete fantasy examples target roughly age seven. No formal reading-age certification is claimed. Adult notes are intentionally more advanced.
- The final review reuses the 19 lesson choices as a gentle knowledge-retrieval exercise. It is not a standardized or clinical exam.
- No copied artwork, logos, questionnaires, proprietary manual text, or treatment worksheets are included.

## Expanded story edition (1.1.0)

Each place now includes arrival, an optional discovery, a worked teaching scene, guided rehearsal, and a knowledge choice with a closing scene. The three-page prologue introduces Glaloula as a fairy and Aunt Fern as her caregiver. These fictional examples explain the existing principles; they do not add a prescribed treatment plan.

## Practice edition (1.2.0)

Optional card activities rehearse the existing concepts without real-world assignments. The final gathering uses new fictional situations to review each of the 19 principles. Practice completion measures only game interaction, never mental health.

## Responsive world edition (1.3.0)

Discovery light and garden flowers respond to reading and game choices, not mental-health measures. The four final-review story scenes preserve the distinction between friendship and caregiver-led treatment. No additional treatment techniques or individual care prescriptions are introduced.

## Learning and character edition (1.4.0)

New fictional teacher memories and Glaloula’s letters connect the principles to character goals. Conversation branches show differences between well-intended responses without claiming that a particular sentence reliably reduces distress. Teach-back and new-situation exercises rehearse conceptual understanding; they are not clinical assessments. Own-words practice records no audio or personal text. No real-world treatment tasks are assigned.
